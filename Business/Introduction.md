# Introduction to SOLO Business

## A general talk : Corporate culture

What it teaches:

- How to navigate bureaucracy
- How large-scale enterprise systems handle compliance
- How to maintain legacy codebases
- How to work within specialized silos.

What it does not teach:

- How to go from 0 to 1
- How to find your first customer
- How to price a product
- How to market
- How to handle customer churn.

## A general talk: SOLO one

- A solo builder has to wear every hat: engineer, product manager, designer, marketer, and support agent.
- Not having corporate experience is not a fatal flaw - **lack of market awareness and distribution skills is**.

## A general talk: Product Preparation Thinking

The mistake that leads to bankruptcy and despair is treating product creation like an all-or-nothing gamble.

**The Fatal Approach:** Spending 9 to 12 months in isolation building a massive, complex software/hardware system, launching it to silence, and running out of money.

**The Pragmatic Approach (Iterative Bets):**

1. **Find a painful problem first:** Don't build AI for the sake of AI. Find a niche where people or businesses lose hours of time or money every week.
2. **Ship an MVP in 2-3 weeks:** Build the smallest functional version that solves the core problem.
3. **Talk to users before building:** If you can't get 5 people interested in the concept before writing code, they won't buy it after you write 10,000 lines of code.
4. **Treat each launch as an experiment, not an identity:** If product #1 get zero traction, pivot or move to product #2 without taking it personally.

## A general talk: De-Risking the Financials: the "Barbell strategy"

Going completely solo without any income source creates desparation

Desparation kills good product decisions because you need money tomorrow, while building a sustainable product takes months

To survive without a traditional corporate job:

- **Sell high-skill services/freelancing on the side:** 
  - Build automated AI workflows
  - micro-tools
  - custom software for clients on contract

NOTE: This pays your bills and keeps you in touch with real-world business problems
